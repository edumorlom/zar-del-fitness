# Both policies are mandatory; paperwork completes compliance

Zar del Fitness needs both a Privacy Policy and Terms and Conditions, and in November 2026 Argentine law sets much of what they must say. Ley 25.326 is still the governing law, since no reform bill has passed. It requires that clients be told, before any data is collected and in a visible place, the purpose, the recipients, who the controller is, which answers are optional and how to exercise their rights, with the AAIP legend fixed by Res. AAIP 14/2018 ([Boletín Oficial](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/179914/20180309)). Ley 24.240 art. 38 requires a business that sells through adhesion contracts to **publish the model contract on its website** ([Ley 24.240](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/638/actualizacion)), and Disp. 954/2025 requires a "Botón de arrepentimiento" and a "Botón de baja de servicio" on the first screen ([Disp. 954/2025](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-417152/texto)). The portal stores health data, which the law expressly treats as sensitive, along with progress photos, DNI and fingerprints, all on US providers that the AAIP does not consider adequate. The documents therefore only hold up if they come with separate express consents, an express consent to the US transfer, a way into the gym that does not use fingerprints, and a specific, revocable consent for every marketing image. The website changes shipping with this report cover most of the website duties. The main gaps left on the website are a consent to the US transfer, the Data Fiscal QR, a block identifying the provider, a withdrawal notice above the Pay button, prices displayed as tax rules require, analytics consent and a disclosure in the AI chat. The larger exposure is offline: registering the databases with the AAIP through TAD, the ARCA Data Fiscal, a profesor de educación física with a CABA-recognised title entered in the Registro de Gimnasios, a Railway DPA that declares "sensitive data: none", an AI receptionist vendor about which no public information was found, and consent for client photos that are already published. The money at stake is lopsided. AAIP fines run from ARS 1,000 to 100,000 per infraction, while a March 2026 injury judgment against a gym chain reached about **ARS 85 million** ([Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/judiciales/2026/03/23/grave-accidente-en-un-gimnasio-condenaron-a-una-cadena-deportiva-a-pagar-una-indemnizacion-millonaria-a-un-socio/)). The research could not open official legal texts directly, so the lawyer should check the points in the last section before anything is published.

## Ley 25.326 still governs, and health data sets the bar

Argentina has not replaced its 2000 data protection law. **Ley 25.326 and Decreto 1558/2001 remain fully in force**, and one analysis notes that the 2026 reform bill "no genera obligaciones actuales" ([abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/index.php/nuevo-proyecto-de-ley-de-proteccion-de-datos-personales/39762)). The Executive's 2023 overhaul lost parliamentary status and returned on 16 July 2026 as bill 3397-D-2026, led by Deputy Agustín Rossi. As of 26 September 2026 it had no committee report and no floor vote ([HCDN](https://www2.hcdn.gob.ar/proyectos/proyecto.jsp?exp=3397-D-2026); [Allende & Brea](https://allende.com/privacidad-y-ciberseguridad/se-reintroduce-en-el-congreso-el-proyecto-de-reforma-integral-del-regimen-argentino-de-proteccion-de-datos-personales-08-12-2026/)). A rival bill, 1751-D-2026, would also replace the law and adds rules on AI ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/se-impulsa-un-nuevo-proyecto-de-reforma-del-r-gimen-de-protecci-n-de-datos-en-argentina)). Argentina has been a party to Convention 108 since 1 June 2019. It approved Convention 108+ by Ley 27.699 and deposited its ratification on 17 April 2023 ([Palabras del Derecho](https://www.palabrasdelderecho.com.ar/articulo/4228/Argentina-ratifico-el-Convenio-108-sobre-proteccion-de-datos); [Marval](https://www.marval.com/publicacion/argentina-ratifica-el-convenio-108-15441?lang=en)). In practice, Zar should draft under Ley 25.326, the decree and AAIP resolutions, and cite no bill as binding. Features the bills would require, such as data portability, breach notice and human review of automated decisions, are cheap to adopt now and future-proof the policy.

### The privacy notice is a legal duty, not a courtesy

Art. 6 requires that data subjects be informed "previamente … en forma expresa y clara" of five things: the purpose and the recipients; that the database exists, and the identity and address of its controller; whether answers are mandatory or optional, especially for sensitive data; the consequences of providing data, refusing to, or giving inaccurate data; and the rights of access, rectification and suppression ([Ley 25.326, InfoLEG](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/60000-64999/64790/texact.htm)). **Res. AAIP 14/2018 repealed the old DNPDP Disp. 10/2008.** It requires controllers to "exhibir en forma clara y expresa, en un sitio visible" that information before collection, "con especial mención a la forma en que pueden ejercerse los derechos" ([Marval](https://www.marval.com/Publicacion/nuevas-resoluciones-en-materia-de-proteccion-de-datos-personales-13167?lang=es)). Its legend names the AAIP, "en su carácter de Órgano de Control de la Ley N° 25.326", as the body that "tiene la atribución de atender las denuncias y reclamos". This wording is live on Visa Argentina's site ([Visa Argentina](https://www.visa.com.ar/supporting-info/proteccion-datos-personales.html)). Any template that still names the defunct Dirección Nacional is out of date.

Res. 14/2018, unlike its predecessor, does not say where the notice must appear. The conservative reading is to put a short notice with a link to the policy at every point where data is collected: signup, the intake form, fingerprint enrolment, the newsletter form and the chat widget, not only the footer. Google's Analytics terms separately require a posted privacy policy that discloses GA and its cookies ([Google Analytics Terms](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7124332?hl=en-uk)). A privacy policy is therefore effectively mandatory twice over.

### Health data is sensitive, so consent must be separate, express and recorded

Art. 2 defines "datos sensibles" to include "información referente a la salud", and art. 7.1 states that "Ninguna persona puede ser obligada a proporcionar datos sensibles" ([InfoLEG](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/60000-64999/64790/texact.htm)). Art. 8 lets "establecimientos sanitarios" and "profesionales vinculados a las ciencias de la salud" process their patients' health data. A gym is neither.

Zar therefore relies on two legal bases. For in-person members, CABA requires a declaración de aptitud física (covered in the CABA section below). That supports processing that minimum as a legal obligation under arts. 5.2.b and 7.2. Everything beyond it rests on consent: extra medical history, body-fat and visceral-fat figures, "biological age" and progress photos. For online clients outside CABA, all health data rests on consent.

Art. 5.1 requires consent that is "libre, expreso e informado". It must be given in writing "o por otro medio que permita se le equipare", and when it is given together with other declarations it must appear "en forma expresa y destacada" after the art. 6 notice ([InfoLEG](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/60000-64999/64790/texact.htm)). Decreto 1558/2001 art. 5 adds that informed consent follows an explanation "en forma adecuada a su nivel social y cultural" ([ARCA biblioteca](https://biblioteca.afip.gob.ar/dcp/DEC_C_001558_2001_11_29)). According to the consolidated text, consent can be withdrawn at any time, without retroactive effect; the research could not re-read that sentence ([Decreto 1558/2001](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/70000-74999/70368/norma.htm)). Some data needs no consent. Contract data (name, contact details, bookings, payments) falls under art. 5.2.d, and DNI together with name and date of birth fits the art. 5.2.c list exception. Marketing, health and biometric data fit neither exception.

The portal consent screen in this release has the structure the law requires: a separate express health consent, optional consents for fingerprint, image use and marketing, and an append-only log that records the policy version. That log is the evidence that makes an electronic click "equiparable" to a signature.

Four refinements matter. First, the health consent should name progress photos and body-composition metrics explicitly. Second, art. 6.d requires stating what happens if the client refuses, and the answer differs by client. In-person members cannot train in the gym without the CABA declaration. Online clients get less personalised programming. Third, because of art. 7.1, optional health fields must not become a condition of portal or coaching access beyond what law or safety genuinely needs. Fourth, withdrawing a consent should be logged and should be as easy as giving it.

### Fingerprints are lawful with consent and a real alternative

AAIP Res. 4/2019 is binding despite its "criterios orientadores" label. It treats biometric identifiers as ordinary personal data unless they reveal more about the person. One extract renders the criterion as saying biometric data "no serán nunca considerados datos sensibles a los efectos de la LPDP, salvo que de su análisis surja información cuyo uso pueda resultar potencialmente discriminatorio para su titular" ([abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/mejores-practicas-en-la-aplicacion-de-la-ley-de-proteccion-de-datos-personales-resolucion-42019-de-la-agencia-de-acceso-a-la-informacion-publica/23000); [Marval](https://www.marval.com/publicacion/mejores-practicas-en-la-aplicacion-de-la-ley-de-proteccion-de-datos-personales-13318)). The AAIP's later reasoning leans the other way. The recitals of Res. 255/2022 note that the Convention 108+ framework "amplía la definición de datos sensibles, incluyendo datos genéticos y biométricos" ([Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/argentina-the-data-protection-authoritys-resolution-no-2552022-on-genetic-data)).

The research found no Argentine decision on fingerprint readers in gyms. The nearest precedent is Spain. The AEPD fined the Metropolitan gym **€27,000** for requiring fingerprints to enter ([Confilegal](https://confilegal.com/20240213-el-gimnasio-metropolitan-sancionado-con-27-000-euros-por-pedir-la-huella-dactilar-para-acceder-a-las-instalaciones/)). In another case it held that consent obtained through house rules was invalid because "no se ofrece una alternativa distinta real y efectiva al uso de la huella" ([AEPD PS/00002/2018](https://www.aepd.es/documento/ps-00002-2018.pdf)).

A design that satisfies both Argentine readings combines four elements: optional, express consent; an equivalent card, PIN or QR alternative at the door; storage of templates rather than images; and deletion of the template when the membership ends. The optional fingerprint consent in the portal is only valid if that alternative actually exists at reception.

### Registration, rights and security run on fixed deadlines

Art. 21 requires registration in the Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos Personales. Decreto 1558/2001 art. 1 reads this broadly, to cover any database "que exceden el uso exclusivamente personal" ([Decreto 1558/2001](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/70000-74999/70368/norma.htm)). Registration runs through TAD. The controller registers first, with clave fiscal level 2 or higher, and then declares each database. The registration does not expire, but it must be updated whenever anything declared changes ([argentina.gob.ar](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/inscribir-un-responsable-de-bases-de-datos-personales-privadas)). Failing to register is an infraction under Res. 126/2024 ([AAIP trámites](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/aaip/datospersonales/tramites)).

Requests from clients have short, fixed deadlines. Access requests must be answered within **10 calendar days** of a formal request. Access is free at intervals of at least six months (art. 14) and always free for advertising databases (art. 27.2). Rectification, update or suppression must be done within **5 business days** (art. 16.3). Suppression can be refused where the law requires the data to be kept, as with tax records (art. 16.5) ([InfoLEG](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/60000-64999/64790/texact.htm)). The AAIP has held that a lack of proof of identity does not justify ignoring an access request ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/la-falta-de-acreditacion-de-identidad-no-resulta-suficiente-para-no-contestar-un-pedido-de-acceso-a-datos-personales-2/)), and it fined Rappi ARS 80,000 for not handling a deletion request ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/se-sanciona-a-una-empresa-por-no-atender-el-derecho-a-la-supresion-de-los-datos-personales)).

On security, art. 9 requires adequate technical and organisational measures. Res. AAIP 47/2018 sets out *recommended* measures and suggests reporting incidents to incidente.seguridad@aaip.gob.ar ([Boletín Oficial](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/188654/20180725?busqueda=1); [Marval](https://www.marval.com/Publicacion/fuerza-de-seguridad-es-sancionada-por-un-incidente-de-seguridad-13517)). No statute requires breach notification. Even so, in the Cencosud case the AAIP concluded that the controller should have reported proactively ([abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/multa-por-data-breach-en-argentina/29132)). Art. 4.7 requires data to be destroyed once it is no longer needed, so the policy must state a retention period for each category of data.

### Every marketing email must state the right to opt out

Decreto 1558/2001 art. 27 is quoted by Marval as requiring that every advertising communication "por correo, teléfono, correo electrónico, Internet u otro medio a distancia" indicate "en forma expresa y destacada, la posibilidad del titular del dato de solicitar el retiro o bloqueo, total o parcial, de su nombre de la base de datos" ([Marval](https://www.marval.com/publicacion/proteccion-de-datos-personales----derecho-de-bloqueo-5534)). Disp. DNPDP 4/2009 adds that advertising the recipient did not request or consent to must carry the single word "publicidad" in the email header ([abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/ejercicio-del-derecho-de-retiro-o-bloqueo-opt-out/2805); [Disp. 4/2009](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-151221/texto)). Failing to remove a name and email address from an advertising database on request is a listed infraction ([Marval on Res. 126/2024](https://www.marval.com/publicacion/novedades-en-la-clasificacion-de-infracciones-y-en-el-regimen-de-graduacion-de-sanciones-16832)).

The unsubscribe link in this release provides the mechanism. Each marketing email should also carry the statement itself, worded around art. 27 inc. 3 of the law. Telephone marketing is governed separately by the "No Llame" registry, which advertisers must check before calling ([Boletín Oficial](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/308122/20240524)).

### Cookies, the AI receptionist and minors fall under the general rules

Argentina has no cookie statute, and the AAIP has issued no cookie guidance ([CookieHub](https://www.cookiehub.com/pdpa-argentina)). Analytics identifiers therefore follow the ordinary notice and consent rules.

There is no AI statute either. The AAIP created an AI transparency programme by Res. 161/2023 ([Boletín Oficial](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/293363/20230904)). In 2024 it published a guide recommending impact assessments before deployment, minimisation, traceability and transparency ([argentina.gob.ar](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/guia-de-la-aaip-para-usar-la-inteligencia-artificial-de-manera-responsable)). The guide carries no sanctions of its own, but Ley 25.326 applies in full to the client data the chatbot reads.

For minors, Res. 4/2019 bases consent on the Civil Code's rules of progressive autonomy (arts. 26 and 639). It requires only "mejores esfuerzos" to confirm that whoever consents for a minor holds parental responsibility ([Marval](https://www.marval.com/publicacion/mejores-practicas-en-la-aplicacion-de-la-ley-de-proteccion-de-datos-personales-13318)). The age of 13 that is often cited is one commentator's reading, not a rule.

### Fines are small in pesos; suspension and complaints are the real cost

Res. AAIP 126/2024 grades infractions in three tiers ([abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/resolucion-1262024-aaip-cambios-en-la-clasificacion-de-infracciones-a-las-leyes-nro-25326-y-26951-y-en-el-regimen-de-graduacion-de-sanciones/34897)). When several infractions arising from the same conduct are sanctioned together, the total is capped at **500 times the top of the scale**, which means ARS 40M, 45M or 50M ([Beccar Varela](https://beccarvarela.com/novedades/ley-de-proteccion-de-datos-y-ley-registro-no-llame-modificacion-del-regimen-sancionatorio/)). Paying voluntarily within 20 business days halves the fine.

| Grade | Fine (ARS) | Other sanctions |
|---|---|---|
| Leve | 1,000–80,000 | up to 2 warnings |
| Grave | 80,001–90,000 | up to 4 warnings; suspension 1–30 days |
| Muy grave | 90,001–100,000 | up to 6 warnings; suspension 31–365 days; closure or cancellation of the database |

Suspension is the sharper tool. On 2 November 2026 the AAIP announced that it had suspended the database of 5 ON LINE S.R.L. over its debt-collection practices. In the same announcement it reported sanctioning another company for not supplying information during an investigation into a deletion request ([argentina.gob.ar](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/la-aaip-sanciono-empresas-por-incumplimientos-la-ley-ndeg-25326)). For a gym, the realistic triggers are an ex-client's unanswered access or deletion request, marketing sent after an opt-out, and an unanswered AAIP inquiry. The policy and the internal procedures should be built around those three.

## Consumer law makes the terms public, cancellable and refundable

The research found no law or decree that changed the substantive text of Ley 24.240 in 2024–2026. The changes came through regulations, and two of them share the number 377. Disp. 954/2025 created the two buttons. Disp. SSDCyLC 377/2026 (11 March 2026) issued a new list of abusive clauses. Decreto 377/2026 (22 May 2026) lets an offender who does not appeal pay 50% of a fine within 10 business days ([Boletín Oficial](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/342265/20260522); [argentina.gob.ar](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/disposici%C3%B3n-377-2026-423801/texto)).

### Publish the contract and identify the provider before checkout

Art. 38 requires anyone selling to consumers through adhesion contracts to "publicar en su sitio web un ejemplar del modelo de contrato a suscribir". The premises must display a sign reading "Se encuentra a su disposición un ejemplar del modelo de contrato que propone la empresa a suscribir al momento de la contratación" ([Ley 24.240](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/638/actualizacion)). Art. 10 requires a written contract "en idioma castellano, en forma completa, clara y fácilmente legible, sin reenvíos a textos o documentos que no se entreguen previa o simultáneamente". The Spanish version of the bilingual terms should therefore be declared the governing text for Argentine consumers, and it must stand on its own.

Res. SCI 270/2020, which brought Mercosur GMC Res. 37/19 into Argentine law, has applied since 7 March 2021. It requires the business to show, "en ubicación de fácil visualización y previo a la formalización del contrato", its trade and legal names, its physical and email addresses and a consumer-service email ([Erreius](https://www.erreius.com/Legislacion/documento/20200908073037576/resolucion-270-2020-incorporacion-al-ordenamiento-juridico-nacional-de-la-resolucion-37-2019-del-grupo-mercado-comun-del-mercosur-relativa-a-la-proteccion-al-consumidor-en-el-comercio-electronico); [LT10](https://www.lt10.com.ar/noticia/279421--nueva-normativa-para-el-comercio-electronico-)). Art. 33 of Ley 24.240 forbids publishing a PO box as the address. Zar needs an identification block (titular or razón social, CUIT, Av. Álvarez Thomas 847, email and WhatsApp) on the legal pages and on the checkout screen.

### Two buttons, a 24-hour code and a 72-hour receipt

Disp. 954/2025 (Boletín Oficial, 4 September 2025) **repealed Res. 316/2018 and Res. 424/2020**, so templates that cite those resolutions are outdated ([Tavarone](https://tavarone.com/obligatoriedad-de-implementar-el-boton-de-baja-y-el-boton-de-arrepentimiento-en-canales-digitales-comerciales/)). It provides that businesses selling at a distance "a través de páginas web y/o canales digitales de comercialización o formato similar, deberán tener a simple vista, en lugar destacado y en el primer acceso, un link denominado 'BOTÓN DE BAJA DE SERVICIO'". The "Botón de arrepentimiento" follows the same rule. The business "no podrá requerir al consumidor registración previa ni ningún otro trámite adicional", and within **24 hours** it must reply through the same medium with an identification code ([Disp. 954/2025](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-417152/texto); [iProfesional](https://www.iprofesional.com/legales/436661-entradas-y-servicios-empresas-deben-implementar-el-boton-de-arrepentimiento-y-de-baja)). The adaptation period ran out around **4 November 2025** ([Nicholson y Cano](https://nicholsonycano.com.ar/alertas-legales/disposicion-ministerio-de-economia-954-2025-nueva-reglamentacion-sobre-boton-de-arrepentimiento-y-boton-de-baja-de-servicio/)).

Whether a one-off prepaid Premium pass without automatic debit falls within the cancellation-button rule is unclear. Including the button, as this release does, makes the question moot. The release's form stores the request and emails a code. Because the rule refers to a reply through the same medium, showing the code on screen when the form is submitted is a cheap extra safeguard.

On top of the regulation sit two statutory duties. Under art. 10 ter, a service contracted electronically can be cancelled "mediante el mismo medio utilizado en la contratación", and the company must send "una constancia fehaciente dentro de las 72 horas" ([Juristeca CABA](https://juristeca.jusbaires.gob.ar/?p=2477)). A CABA court confirmed an ARS 80,000 fine on a telephone company for breaching this article. Art. 10 quater bans charging "preaviso, mes adelantado y/o cualquier otro concepto" when a client cancels ([Ley 24.240](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/638/actualizacion)). Plans contracted over WhatsApp must therefore be cancellable over WhatsApp, and the cancellation email must serve as the 72-hour receipt.

### Ten days to withdraw, with no "service started" exception

CCyC art. 1110 gives consumers in distance contracts the "derecho irrenunciable de revocar la aceptación dentro de los diez días", and clauses that make revocation impossible "se tienen por no escritos". Art. 1111 requires the right to be stated "en caracteres destacados … ubicada como disposición inmediatamente anterior a la firma". The right does not lapse if the consumer was not properly informed of it. Art. 1115 makes revocation free of cost ([InfoLEG CCyC](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/235000-239999/235975/norma.htm); [codigocivilonline](https://codigocivilonline.com.ar/?p=2491)).

Art. 1116 lists the exceptions: custom or perishable goods, unsealed recordings and downloadable files, and the daily press. Unlike EU law, **it has no exception for services that have already started** ([codigocivilonline](https://codigocivilonline.com.ar/articulo-1116/)). A blanket "no refunds once the pass begins" clause is therefore void during the first ten days. The research found no authority on whether days actually used in that window may be deducted.

The portal checkout needs the withdrawal notice in highlighted text immediately above the Pay button, and an electronic purchase confirmation sent "sin demora" (art. 1108). Refunds should go back through Mercado Pago with no fees deducted. Its Checkout Pro documentation confirms that disputed amounts are held during a chargeback and returned to the buyer if the chargeback is upheld ([Mercado Pago Developers](https://www.mercadopago.com.ar/developers/es/docs/checkout-pro/chargebacks)).

### Prices must show the final peso amount, and sometimes the untaxed amount

Res. SIC 4/2025 repealed the old price-display rules (Res. 7/2002 and 55/2002). It requires the **final price in pesos**, meaning the total the consumer pays ([Boletín Oficial](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/319787/20250117)). Prices "además de ser exhibidos en pesos, podrán exhibirse en dólares estadounidenses", and services "desde, hacia y en el exterior podrán ser exhibidos y publicitados exclusivamente en dólares estadounidenses". The USD 100 international plan can therefore be shown in dollars only if it is sold to clients abroad. If residents of Argentina can buy it, it also needs an ARS price.

Since 1 April 2025 the rule has also required the net price without national indirect taxes, under the legend "**PRECIO SIN IMPUESTOS NACIONALES**" ([argentina.gob.ar explainer](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/exhibicion_de_precios_resolucion_4_2025.pdf)). Two points remain open. Media reports say that online, both prices must use the same font size, which conflicts with the "smaller characters" rule for shops ([HOOD INT](https://www.int.com.ar/novedades/novedades/transparencia-fiscal-en-tiendas-catalogos-online-lo-que-exige-la-resolucion-4-2025/)). It is also unclear whether a monotributista who issues "C" invoices must show the net price at all. One billing blog, citing no rule, says the invoice change reaches only Responsables Inscriptos ([evemuriel](https://evemuriel.com/blog/facturacion/transparencia-fiscal-facturas-con-iva-detallado.html)). This is why the owner's tax status has to be settled before the price display is finalised.

CCyC art. 1098 bars price differences based on nationality, so the USD price should depend on where the client is, not on their passport. Disp. 377/2026 lists clauses that let the business change the contract unilaterally as abusive ([Allende & Brea](https://allende.com/privacidad-y-ciberseguridad/nuevo-listado-de-clausulas-abusivas-en-contratos-de-consumo-03-18-2026/)). That matters because price increases are what regulators fine gyms for most often.

### Data Fiscal and consumer-defence links complete the footer

ARCA's current page still states that "los sitios web de quienes realicen operaciones de venta de cosas muebles, locaciones y prestaciones de obras y/o servicios … deberán colocar en un lugar visible de su página principal, el logo Formulario 960/D" ([ARCA](https://www.afip.gob.ar/960/formulario-960/obligados.asp)). The form, created by RG 4042-E/2017, is registered with clave fiscal ([RG 4042-E/2017](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-274424/texto)). It must be renewed whenever the business's tax status changes ([ARCA FAQ](https://servicioscf.afip.gob.ar/publico/abc/ABCpaso2.aspx?id_nivel1=562&id_nivel2=1872&p=Data+fiscal)). None of the coverage of ARCA's 2025 deregulation mentions the Data Fiscal.

Two consumer-defence links are also required. Res. SCI 274/2021 requires websites to link to the national Ventanilla Única Federal (VUF) complaint form with the text "Defensa de las y los Consumidores. Para reclamos ingrese aquí" ([abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/defensa-del-consumidor-obligacion-de-establecer-un-enlace-link-a-la-ventanilla-unica-federal/28038)). Decreto 55/2025 dissolved COPREC from 1 February 2025, so templates that mention it are obsolete ([Beccar Varela](https://beccarvarela.com/en/novedades/decreto-55-2025-disoluciones/)). CABA joined the VUF through Disp. 423/DGDYPC/2025 ([BO CABA](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/775277)), and the City's guidance under its Ley 2244 says businesses must link to its Defensa al Consumidor office on their websites ([buenosaires.gob.ar](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/gcaba_historico/informacion-para-comerciantes)).

### What the terms cannot say, and what they should say instead

Ley 24.240 art. 37 treats as not agreed any clause that would "desnaturalicen las obligaciones o limiten la responsabilidad por daños". CCyC art. 1743 invalidates clauses that exempt or limit liability "cuando afectan derechos indisponibles, atentan contra la buena fe … o son abusivas" ([InfoLEG CCyC](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/235000-239999/235975/norma.htm)). Disp. 377/2026 adds a list of abusive clauses. It includes clauses that deem the contract accepted just by browsing, allow use of personal data outside Ley 25.326, shift caso fortuito onto the consumer, penalise negative reviews, let the business rescind without cause, or set a jurisdiction other than the consumer's domicile ([abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/defensa-del-consumidor-actualizacion-del-regimen-de-clausulas-abusivas-en-contratos-de-consumo/38725)). CCyC art. 1109 voids forum clauses in distance contracts, and art. 2654 bars any choice of forum in international consumer contracts. The terms should impose CABA courts on no one, least of all foreign clients paying in USD.

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "Zar del Fitness no se responsabiliza por lesiones" or "el cliente entrena bajo su exclusivo riesgo" | The client's duty to declare their health truthfully, follow instructions and stop if symptoms appear; the inherent risks of training, presented as information; the gym's own safety commitments |
| "No hay reembolsos bajo ninguna circunstancia" | Withdrawal within 10 days with a full refund; after day 10, the current prepaid month is not refunded and nothing further is owed; a refund or credit when Zar cannot provide the service |
| "Las bajas solo se aceptan en forma presencial" or "preaviso de 30 días" | Cancellation through the same channel used to sign up (portal, button, WhatsApp), a receipt within 72 hours, no charges |
| "Podemos modificar precios y condiciones en cualquier momento"; "el uso continuado implica aceptación" | Price changes apply only to future purchases, are announced in advance, and never re-price periods already paid |
| "Tribunales de la Ciudad de Buenos Aires con exclusión de cualquier otro fuero" | No forum clause, or the courts of the consumer's domicile |
| "No nos responsabilizamos por objetos personales" | A recommendation not to bring valuables, plus a description of the lockers provided |

## CABA gym rules and liability law limit what the terms can promise

### A sworn health declaration replaced the annual apto médico

CABA Ley 139 still governs gyms. **Ley 6316 (2020)** rewrote its art. 3: "todas las personas que realicen actividades físicas no competitivas en el gimnasio, deberán presentar una declaración de aptitud física". The CABA Ministerio de Salud sets the content, based on the PAR-Q questionnaire, with separate forms for minors and adults ([BO CABA, Ley 6316](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/524323); [RTN](https://rtn.com.ar/eliminan-la-obligacion-de-presentar-un-certificado-medico-para-ingresar-a-los-gimnasios-en-caba/)). Decreto 11/2022 regulates it as a sworn health self-report ([BO CABA](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/582960)).

The consolidated text of Ley 139 contains the new art. 3 but **still has an art. 10 referring to a "certificado médico de aptitud física (APTO MÉDICO)"** ([Digesto CABA](https://documentosboletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/publico/PE-DIS-MJYSGC-DGLYTAGC-6-20-ANX-23.pdf)). Market practice reflects the confusion. Megatlon asks CABA members for an annual digital sworn declaration ([Megatlon](https://megatlon.com/dudas/)), while SportClub calls the medical certificate a "condición esencial para la contratación" ([SportClub](https://eks-production-01-asociate-imagenes.s3.amazonaws.com/MODELO_NUEVO_2025_TYC_PLAN_FLEX_MENSUAL_WEB.pdf)). The defensible course is to collect the official CABA declaration at signup and again every year and whenever the client's health changes. As contract policy, Zar can also require a medical certificate when the questionnaire flags a risk. The terms should not cite "Ley 139" as the source of any requirement until the lawyer confirms the current text.

### A titled profesor and a registry entry are preconditions

Ley 139 art. 2 requires that gym activities "deben ser supervisadas por un/a profesor/a de Educación Física con título reconocido por la Ciudad de Buenos Aires". The only exemption, added by Ley 6616, covers hotel gyms used solely by guests, not boutique studios ([BO CABA, Ley 139](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/1278)). The City must keep "un registro de todos los gimnasios y de los profesionales responsables". Disp. 417-DGHP-06 created the Registro de Gimnasios, which records the name, DNI and title of the responsible profesor, and **any change of responsible profesor must be reported** ([BO CABA, Ley 139](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/1278)). If the people who supervise VIP sessions do not hold that title, this is a direct Ley 139 gap that no website wording can fix. The law governs physical premises in CABA, not online coaching for clients elsewhere.

### Defibrillators are required for gyms nationally, but CABA's rule is unclear

National Decreto 402/2022, which regulates Ley 27.159, expressly lists "gimnasios" among covered spaces. It requires trained staff, signage, a national registry of defibrillators (DEA) and ANMAT-approved devices, paid for by owners of private spaces ([Decreto 402/2022](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/368050/texto)). A press report about two years old said no jurisdiction had adhered to the law ([Primera Edición](https://www.primeraedicion.com.ar/nota/100915746/muerte-subita-ninguna-provincia-adhirio-al-desfibrilador-obligatorio/)). CABA's Ley 4077 requires a DEA only in spaces of "concurrencia masiva" or high risk ([Defensoría CABA](https://defen-dev.defensoria.org.ar/normativas-cdh/ley-4077-obligatoriedad-de-contar-con-desfibrilador-externo-automatico-en-lugares-de-concurrencia-masiva/)), and the research found no threshold for that term. Whether a small studio is legally bound remains open. The liability analysis below makes a DEA, trained staff and an emergency medical service worth having regardless.

### Waivers are void; evidence and insurance are the real defences

Courts treat gym membership as a consumer relationship carrying an objective **duty of safety**. The member proves the damage and the causal link, and the gym must then prove a cause that exonerates it ([Microjuris, Neuquén](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2017/03/20/relacion-de-consumo-entre-el-afiliado-que-se-lesiono-en-una-maquina-y-el-gimnasio/)). In March 2026 a civil appeals court confirmed an indemnity of about **ARS 85 million** against a gym chain. It held that accepting the inherent risks of exercise does not exempt the gym when the damage comes from factors outside the victim's conduct. It rejected punitive damages and enforced the policy's insurance coverage cap ([iProfesional](https://www.iprofesional.com/legales/450857-fallo-clave-gimnasio-pagara-indemnizacion-millonaria-por-accidente-cliente)).

Gyms win when they can point to the client's own conduct and sound facilities. In July 2025 CNCiv Sala F reversed a judgment because the injury came from the plaintiff tripping over another student and no facility failure was proven ([Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/judiciales/2025/07/16/revocaron-una-condena-contra-un-gimnasio-por-la-caida-de-una-mujer-durante-una-clase-de-entrenamiento/)). By contrast, a Corrientes court held a gym liable for a client's fracture in May 2026 ([El Litoral](https://www.ellitoral.com.ar/sociedad/2026-5-8-16-48-0-condenan-a-un-gimnasio-de-corrientes-por-la-fractura-que-sufrio-una-clienta)).

The terms should therefore assign duties and build evidence rather than attempt waivers. That means a signed health declaration, a duty to follow instructions and report symptoms, a requirement that online clients train with safe equipment in a safe space, and a recommendation to take out personal accident insurance. Behind the terms, Zar needs civil liability insurance and kept records of attendance, equipment maintenance and staff titles.

### Nutrition advice, supplements and the traumatólogo need firewalls

Ley 24.301 reserves the profession of licenciado en nutrición "en el ámbito de la Capital Federal" to degree holders ([Ley 24.301](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-24301-697/texto)). Código Penal art. 208 punishes anyone without a title who habitually prescribes "cualquier medio destinado al tratamiento de las enfermedades de las personas" ([Código Penal](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/15000-19999/16546/texact.htm)). Coaching copy should therefore offer general guidance on eating habits ("orientación general de hábitos alimentarios"), avoid terms like "plan nutricional", "dieta" or "prescripción", and state that the coach is neither a nutritionist nor a doctor. For supplements at the drinks bar, CAA art. 1381 inc. 9 bars "indicaciones terapéuticas" on labels and in advertising. ANMAT Disp. 4980/2005 forbids, among other things, presenting a supplement as a meal replacement ([ANMAT](https://www.anmat.gob.ar/webanmat/Legislacion/NormasGenerales/Disposicion_ANMAT_4980-2005.pdf)).

When a licensed traumatólogo attends clients, Ley 26.529 applies. The patient owns the historia clínica, the professional is its custodian, and the record must be kept for at least 10 years ([Ley 26.529](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/160000-164999/160432/texact.htm)). The doctor should therefore practise as an independent professional with their own records, consent forms and malpractice insurance. The gym should receive only what the client separately authorises, such as fitness to train or restrictions, and clinical notes should never enter the gym's database.

### Before/after photos need specific, revocable consent

CCyC art. 53 requires consent to capture or reproduce a person's image. Art. 55 adds that such consent "no se presume, es de interpretación restrictiva, y libremente revocable" ([leyfacil](https://leyfacil.com.ar/?p=210)), and doctrine holds it "estrictamente limitado a la finalidad o circunstancias en que se presta" ([Microjuris](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2022/06/22/doctrina-la-proteccion-de-la-imagen-en-el-ambito-virtual/)). Ley 11.723 art. 31 requires **express** consent to put a portrait on the market. The person can revoke it, paying compensation for any damage caused ([Ley 11.723](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)). CNCiv Sala B held that a photo being visible on social media does not authorise a third party to publish it ([Diario Judicial](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-86393-derecho-a-la-imagen-en-redes-sociales-de-empresas)).

Three consequences follow. Progress photos taken for tracking cannot be reused in ads under the tracking consent, and transformation photos shown next to body-fat figures are also health data. Every before/after image already published needs documented consent covering the channels, whether the face, name and figures are shown, and for how long, or it should come down. Images of minors carry the extra protection of Ley 26.061 art. 22 ([Ley 26.061](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/110000-114999/110778/norma.htm)) and should stay out of marketing.

Claims carry a separate risk. DNU 274/2019 art. 11 prohibits advertising that "mediante inexactitudes u ocultamientos pueda inducir a error", with fines of up to 10,000,000 Unidades Móviles ([DNU 274/2019](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/decreto-274-2019-322236/texto)). Ley 24.240 art. 8 makes an ad's promises part of the contract. A headline like "baja 10 kg en 30 días" is therefore both potentially misleading advertising and a binding promise.

## US hosting requires express consent, and foreign clients bring their own law

### The US is not adequate, and vendor DPAs ignore Argentina

Ley 25.326 art. 12 prohibits transfers to countries without adequate protection. Decreto 1558/2001 art. 12 provides that the prohibition "no rige cuando el titular de los datos hubiera consentido expresamente la cesión" ([Decreto 1558/2001](https://docs.argentina.justia.com/federales/decretos/decreto-n-1558-2001-nov-3-2001.pdf)). The AAIP list of adequate countries, under Disp. 60-E/2016 and Res. 34/2019, covers the EU/EEA, the UK, Switzerland, Canada's private sector, Uruguay, New Zealand, Israel and a few others. **The United States is not on it** ([Disp. 60-E/2016](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/disposici%C3%B3n-60-2016-267922/actualizacion); [Legal Core Group](https://legalcoregroup.com.ar/en/international-personal-data-transfers-in-argentina/)).

The contractual alternative is the 2016 AAIP model clauses or the Ibero-American (RIPD) clauses adopted by Res. AAIP 198/2023 ([Res. 198/2023](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/390000-394999/391538/norma.htm)). A contract that does not follow the models must be filed with the AAIP within 30 days, unless it reflects their essential protections ([Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/en/insights/data-protected/data-protected---argentina)). None of the US vendors' DPAs found mentions Argentina. They are built on the EU standard contractual clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

Accepting each DPA is still worthwhile, but **express consent to the US transfer is the only route Zar can put in place on its own**. That consent is missing from the consent screen as described. It belongs on its own unticked line, naming the US as the main destination.

### Railway and Digui are the two weak links

Railway holds everything in the portal, including health data, measurements, photos, DNI and payments. Its only DPA declares that it processes no sensitive data, the most concrete contractual gap the research found. The owner should put the health-data question to Railway in writing and choose and document a hosting region. EU West places the data in an AAIP-adequate country, although Railway remains a US company with US subprocessors. Health fields, photos and DNI should be encrypted at the application level, and a migration plan is needed if Railway's answer is no.

Digui reads bookings, evaluations and payments for logged-in clients. Evaluations are body-composition data, so this is health data. The research found no public information on Digui's operator, its country, its underlying model providers or how long it keeps transcripts. Ley 25.326 art. 25 requires a processor contract covering confidentiality, security and a ban on secondary use, and the AAIP's AI guide recommends an impact assessment before deployment. Until due diligence produces a DPA, a commitment not to train on client data and read-only database views limited to the stated tables, Digui should not have access to evaluation data. The chat widget should say that an AI is answering and how to reach a human.

### Mercado Pago and Google Analytics have defined roles

Mercado Pago (Mercado Libre S.R.L.) names itself controller of its users' data in its own privacy declaration, and sellers receive only the payment and the buyer's email ([MP privacy](https://www.mercadopago.com.ar/privacidad/declaracion-privacidad/30988); [MP buyer protection](https://www.mercadopago.com.ar/ayuda/protecci%C3%B3n-a-compradores_283)). The policy should present it as an independent controller and link to that declaration. No Checkout Pro document expressly sets out how the roles are split, so this point is an inference.

Google's Analytics terms require a policy that discloses GA and its cookies, and visitor consent "where required by law" ([Google Analytics Terms](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7124332?hl=en-uk)). Sources disagree on whether GA4's default retention is 2 or 14 months ([InfoTrust](https://infotrust.com/articles/google-analytics-4-data-retention-settings-101/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge/ga4-data-retention/)), so the property setting should be checked and set to 2 months. Clerk's `__session`, `__client` and `__client_uat` cookies and Cloudflare's `__cf_bm` and `cf_clearance` are documented as strictly necessary ([Clerk](https://clerk.com/docs/guides/how-clerk-works/cookies); [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/reference/policies-compliances/cloudflare-cookies/)). Only GA and any standard YouTube embeds need consent before they load.

### GDPR likely reaches coaching clients in the EU, and Brazil's LGPD reaches Brazilians

Selling online coaching worldwide in USD does not by itself trigger the GDPR's "offering" test. Recital 23 says the mere accessibility of a website is not enough ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). Coaching someone in Spain while tracking their weight, measurements and photos over time is different: it very likely "monitors behaviour" in the EU under art. 3(2)(b). The researchers read the EDPB's territorial-scope guidelines as listing "personalised diet and health analytics services online" as monitoring, but the extract they saw did not show that passage ([EDPB Guidelines 3/2018](https://edpb.europa.eu/sites/edpb/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_3_2018_territorial_scope_en.pdf)).

The European Commission confirmed Argentina's adequacy in January 2024 ([Marval](https://www.marval.com/publicacion/la-comision-europea-ratifica-que-la-argentina-tiene-legislacion-adecuada-para-la-transferencia-internacional-de-datos-personales-15760?lang=en)). That eases transfers from the EU to Argentina, but it does not exempt an Argentine controller that art. 3 reaches directly. Whether to appoint an EU representative under art. 27 is a judgement call, because the "occasional processing" exemption is doubtful when health data is processed regularly.

Other countries reach clients located there in similar ways. Brazil's LGPD art. 3 covers processing of data of individuals in Brazil, wherever the controller is ([Barbieri Advogados](https://barbieriadvogados.com/en/?p=8824)). Uruguay's Ley 18.331 reaches controllers not established there that offer services to its inhabitants ([URCDP](https://www.gub.uy/unidad-reguladora-control-datos-personales/comunicacion/publicaciones/guia-para-cumplimiento-obligaciones-entidades-extranjeras/guia-para-1)). Chile's Ley 21.719, which also applies abroad, starts on 1 December 2026. On 28 October 2026 the Senate approved postponing it to 15 October 2027, but that bill is still before the Chamber of Deputies ([Garrigues](https://www.garrigues.com/es_ES/noticia/chile-redefine-marco-privacidad-ciberseguridad-nuevas-reglas-cumplimiento-cambios)).

In the US, the CCPA's thresholds (USD 26.625 million in revenue or 100,000 consumers) are far out of reach ([Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/navigating-california-consumer-privacy-act-30-essential-faqs-covered-businesses-including-clarifying-regulations-effective-1126)). Washington's My Health My Data Act has no size threshold but applies only if Zar targets Washington residents ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2023/05/washington-state-imposes-far-reaching-privacy-obligations)).

Argentine consumer law follows foreign clients too. CCyC art. 2654 bars any choice of forum in international consumer contracts, and art. 2655 applies the law of the consumer's domicile when the offer was directed to that country ([InfoLEG CCyC](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/235000-239999/235975/norma.htm)). The practical answer is one policy written to GDPR standard, with a legal basis for each purpose, the full list of rights and the right to complain to the client's local authority, rather than arguing country by country that foreign law does not apply.

## Competitors set a low bar, and regulators punish prices and exits

### Chains publish dense PDFs and broad grants

All competitor findings come from search extracts, not full documents, and should be checked against the live pages before anyone relies on them. The big chains publish their terms plan by plan, as dense PDFs and FAQ pages. SportClub's 2025 Plan Flex terms say members can cancel "por el mismo medio" used to sign up, and can revoke web plans within 10 days "a través del botón de arrepentimiento disponible". They call the medical certificate a "condición esencial para la contratación" and treat acceptance as authorisation to capture and reproduce the member's image ([SportClub PDF](https://eks-production-01-asociate-imagenes.s3.amazonaws.com/MODELO_NUEVO_2025_TYC_PLAN_FLEX_MENSUAL_WEB.pdf)). The same extract contains the phrase that the member "podrá ser obligado a suministrar datos sensibles". If the extract is accurate, that sentence inverts art. 7.1.

SportClub's privacy policy says "LA EMPRESA no realiza, en ninguna circunstancia, transferencia internacional de datos personales" ([SportClub privacy](https://app.sportclub.com.ar/legales/Politicas%20de%20Privacidad.html)). Its Google Play listing, meanwhile, describes identification at the door "con biometría y QR" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.muvinai.carnet2&hl=en_US)), and no matching biometric clause was found in the policy. A SportClub franchise contract treats continued use after a price change as acceptance ([SportClub Ushuaia](https://sportclubushuaia.com.ar/legales/SportClub-contrato-de-adhesion.pdf)), which is the reasoning regulators have rejected.

Megatlon (Gimnasios Argentinos S.A.) has the most complete set. It has separate terms and privacy pages, with the privacy page versioned "1.0", and states that its databases "se encuentran registradas en la AGENCIA DE ACCESO A LA INFORMACIÓN PÚBLICA". It gives 30 days' notice of changes to its terms. It also takes a broad licence to use members' image, voice and "reactions" in advertising, which members can revoke by email ([Megatlon T&C](https://megatlon.com/terminos-y-condiciones/); [Megatlon privacy](https://megatlon.com/politicas-de-privacidad/)).

Smart Fit Argentina's FAQ pages are in Portuguese and route cancellations to the front desk or registered letter ([Smart Fit FAQ](https://www.smartfit.com.ar/faqs/192-como-cancelar-o-meu-plano-smart)). It charges a 20% penalty for early cancellation of its Black plan ([Smart Fit FAQ 95](https://www.smartfit.com.ar/faqs/95-tem-multa-para-cancelar-o-plano-black)), and its 12-month online Coach product keeps the same 20% ([Smart Fit Coach](https://www.smartfit.com.ar/coachar)). Fiter requires a 60-day minimum stay and 30 days' notice of cancellation, given in person or by email ([Fiter](https://fiter.com/AR/condiciones)). That notice requirement sits uneasily with art. 10 quater's ban on charging "preaviso".

The +Trainer app is the one peer found that treats health data as a special category under Ley 25.326 ([+Trainer](https://mastrainerapp.com/en/privacy-policy/)). The research found no peer with a biometric clause, a stated retention period, a plain-language summary or a bilingual version.

### Enforcement targets fee increases, extra charges and obstructed exits

The enforcement record is about money and cancellations, not privacy. The national consumer directorate fined Gimnasios Argentinos **ARS 50,000** for not reporting fee increases "en forma cierta, clara y detallada". In 2020 the federal appeals court confirmed the fine and rejected the argument that continued use amounted to consent ([abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/confirman-la-multa-impuesta-a-un-gimnasio-que-no-informo-el-incremento-de-las-cuotas-del-servicio-ofrecido/25970)). A 2016 ruling confirmed a fine for debiting ARS 385 where ARS 285 had been agreed ([Microjuris](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2016/09/20/se-multa-a-un-gimnasio-por-infraccion-a-la-ley-de-defensa-al-consumidor-al-haber-incurrido-en-incumplimiento-contractual-por-cobrar-una-cuota-superior-a-la-convenida/)). In 2025 a CABA appeals court confirmed a fine on the club GEBA for a unilateral extraordinary fee, holding that a sports club counts as a "proveedor" under consumer law ([Microjuris](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2025/10/21/fallos-multa-un-club-deportivo-cobro-una-cuota-extraordinaria-de-manera-unilateral-y-no-prevista-en-el-contrato-de-membresia/)). After a member's bike was stolen from its rack, the CABA consumer authority fined Megatlon ARS 65,000 and ordered ARS 90,859.44 in direct damages, and the appeals court cited its "carácter de reincidente" ([Diario Judicial](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-100335-la-justicia-no-lo-dejo-a-pata)). The consumer association ACYMA is pursuing a collective action against Megatlon over fee increases that were not notified ([ACYMA](https://acyma.org.ar/juicio-megatlon/)).

Complaint sites show the same pattern. Debits continue after members ask to cancel, often because the gym insisted on cancellation in person ([tuquejasuma](https://tuquejasuma.com/megatlon-red-de-clubes/reclamos/megatlon-ladron)). The research found no AAIP action against an Argentine gym over biometrics, health data or marketing photos.

Two lessons follow. Zar's prepaid Premium pass with **no automatic debit already avoids the practice most often sanctioned**, and the terms should say so plainly. And because privacy enforcement against gyms has not yet started, handling health data, fingerprints and photos carefully is a selling point for a premium brand as well as risk management.

## Conclusion

The biggest risks sit in operations, not in the wording of the policies. Data protection fines are small, and the research found no case of them being enforced against an Argentine gym. The exposures that have produced real money and real lawsuits are consumer law (prices, cancellations, refunds) and civil liability for injuries, and both depend on how the gym is run. That puts the most valuable items offline: a titled profesor in the CABA registry, a WhatsApp cancellation procedure that issues receipts, a price rule that never re-prices paid periods, and insurance backed by evidence logs. The privacy work still matters because it is cheap now and expensive later. Health data, photos and DNI currently sit on a host whose contract says it accepts no sensitive data, and an AI vendor of unknown origin can read evaluations. Any complaint, or the passage of either reform bill (both would make portability and human review standard), would turn those paperwork gaps into substantive breaches.

The bilingual pages, the USD plan and remote coaching have quietly turned a CABA gym into an international data controller. One Spanish-governed policy written to GDPR standard, plus an express transfer consent, costs less than arguing country by country that foreign law does not apply. The market bar is low enough that doing this well becomes part of the premium positioning: no competitor found publishes a biometric clause, a retention period, a plain-language summary or a real alternative to fingerprints. The sequence follows from this. First finish the AAIP and ARCA registrations, the Railway question and the Digui due diligence. Only then publish the "registered" statement and the Data Fiscal QR. And have the lawyer clear the verification list before the policy version is frozen in the consent log.
