## 1. CABA gym regime (Ley 139 and amendments): apto físico / declaración, qualified staff, registry, first aid and DEA

### Takeaway
CABA Ley 139 (1998) still governs gyms. **Ley 6316 (2020)** replaced the old mandatory annual medical certificate (apto médico) for *non-competitive* gym activity with a **"declaración de aptitud física"** (a sworn health questionnaire based on the PAR-Q, with separate forms for minors and adults, content set by the CABA Ministerio de Salud). Activities must still be **supervised by a profesor/a de Educación Física with a title recognized by CABA**, and gyms must be in the **Registro de Gimnasios**. On defibrillators, the national Ley 27.159 and Decreto 402/2022 expressly cover gyms. CABA's own Ley 4077 requires a DEA only in places of "concurrencia masiva" (mass attendance), and whether a small boutique gym falls under that is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
**Apto físico / declaración de aptitud física**
- Ley 6316 (CABA, 2020) rewrote art. 3 of Ley 139: "todas las personas que realicen actividades físicas no competitivas en el gimnasio, deberán presentar una declaración de aptitud física", with content set by the Ministerio de Salud and separate forms for minors and adults. Press coverage describes this as removing the mandatory annual medical certificate. The model is based on the "PAR-Q" questionnaire on health and cardiovascular status — [Boletín Oficial CABA, Ley 6316/2020](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/524323); [RTN, "Eliminan la obligación de presentar un certificado médico para ingresar a los gimnasios en CABA"](https://rtn.com.ar/eliminan-la-obligacion-de-presentar-un-certificado-medico-para-ingresar-a-los-gimnasios-en-caba/); [Infobae 13/07/2020](https://www.infobae.com/sociedad/2020/07/13/analizaran-eliminar-el-requisito-de-tener-un-permiso-medico-para-ir-al-gimnasio-en-caba/)
- One secondary summary says that "ante la presencia de patologías" the gym may still ask for a medical certificate. The final ministry form and that rule were **not verified** — [fisicalcheck.com, "Ley de Apto Físico en Buenos Aires"](https://fisicalcheck.com/pages/ley-de-aptitud-fisica) (secondary, commercial site)
- The consolidated Digesto version of Ley 139 contains the new art. 3 (declaración de aptitud física) **but still also has an art. 10 referring to a "certificado médico de aptitud física (APTO MÉDICO)"**. This internal inconsistency means the regime cannot be stated with full certainty from the consolidated text alone — [Digesto CABA, Ley H N° 139 consolidated text (PDF)](https://documentosboletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/publico/PE-DIS-MJYSGC-DGLYTAGC-6-20-ANX-23.pdf) (seen via search snippet only)
- **Superseded rules (do not rely on them):**
  - *Original Ley 139 + Decreto 1821/04, Anexo I, reg. of art. 6:* "Todo Gimnasio deberá exigir a sus usuarios un certificado de aptitud física extendido por profesional médico con título y matrícula habilitada, el cual poseerá una validez de un año a partir de la fecha de su otorgamiento". Gyms had to **keep the certificates, control and demand renewal, and not allow activity without a valid certificate**. The certificate had to state the type of activity allowed — [Ley 139 – Reglamentación (CEDOM)](http://www2.cedom.gob.ar/es/legislacion/normas/leyes/anexos/drl139.html); [Boletín Oficial CABA, Decreto 1821/2004](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/62242)
  - *Ley 5397 (2015):* required a certificate including electrocardiogram results, updated annually. Some sites still cite this; Ley 6316 appears to supersede it — [docmed.ar, "Medicina del Deporte – Apto físico – Marco legal"](https://docmed.ar/medicina-del-deporte-apto-fisico-marco-legal/); [Revista Argentina de Medicina, "El certificado de aptitud física, ¿certifica algo?"](https://www.revistasam.com.ar/index.php/RAM/article/download/297/259/1012)
- **Decreto 11/2022** (CABA) replaced Anexo I of Decreto 1821/04, the regulation of Ley 139. Its content (likely the new declaration regime) was **not verified** — [search snippet of Boletín Oficial CABA, Ley 139 page](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/1278)
- Cardiologists and sports physicians criticized the switch to a sworn declaration — [Página12, "Gimnasios: planean eliminar los aptos médicos"](https://www.pagina12.com.ar/278274-gimnasios-planean-eliminar-los-aptos-medicos/)

**Qualified staff**
- Ley 139 art. 2: "La práctica de actividades físicas o recreativas en los gimnasios, deben ser supervisadas por un/a profesor/a de Educación Física con título reconocido por la Ciudad de Buenos Aires" — [Boletín Oficial CABA, Ley 139](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/1278)
- Art. 2 bis was added by **Ley 6616 (BOCBA 6521, 16/12/2022)**. It exempts gyms in tourist hotels used only by guests, with up to 15 users per shift, from the profesor requirement. There is no exemption for small or boutique gyms — [Digesto CABA, Ley 139 consolidated](https://documentosboletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/publico/PE-DIS-MJYSGC-DGLYTAGC-6-20-ANX-23.pdf) (via snippet)

**Registry**
- Ley 139: "La autoridad de aplicación debe llevar un registro de todos los gimnasios y de los profesionales responsables". It was set up by **Disposición 417-DGHP-06** (Registro de Gimnasios de la CABA, under Ley 139 and Decreto 1821/04). Registration covers the name, DNI and title of the "Profesor/a de Educación Física Responsable Principal de la supervisión", and **any change of responsible profesor must be reported** — [Boletín Oficial CABA, Ley 139](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/1278); [Normativa Gimnasios blog (secondary)](http://normativa-gimnasios.blogspot.com/2008/01/ley-139-gimnasios.html)
- Ley 2575 amended art. 4 of Ley 139; article numbering differs between versions — [Boletín Oficial CABA, Ley 139](https://boletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/normativaba/norma/1278)

**DEA / cardio-protected spaces**
- National **Ley 27.159** (sanctioned 27/07/2015) creates the prevention system for sudden death in public spaces and private spaces open to the public. It requires installing, maintaining and signposting DEAs and training staff — [Matriz Legal, "Ley 27.159: a qué obliga y si está vigente"](https://matrizlegal.org/nacion/ley-27159)
- **Decreto 402/2022** (regulation of Ley 27.159) expressly lists "gimnasios" and "centros e instalaciones deportivas" among covered spaces. Minimum requirements: staff trained and accredited by official institutions, plus signage showing the DEA, its location, instructions and accessibility. It also creates a DEA registry, requires ANMAT-approved devices, and puts the cost on owners of private spaces — [Decreto 402/2022, texto original](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/368050/texto); [Infodeportes, "Se reglamentó la ley 27.159 … en los gimnasios"](https://infodeportes.com.ar/se-reglamento-la-ley-27-159-desfibriladores-externos-automaticos-en-los-gimnasios/); [Res. MSAL 1827/2022, Boletín Oficial](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/272071/20220920)
- A press report (about two years old) said **no jurisdiction had adhered** to Ley 27.159, so its enforcement depended on businesses' goodwill — [Primera Edición](https://www.primeraedicion.com.ar/nota/100915746/muerte-subita-ninguna-provincia-adhirio-al-desfibrilador-obligatorio/)
- **CABA Ley 4077** (sanctioned 01/12/2011, BOCBA 3849, 07/02/2012) requires a DEA in public and private spaces of "concurrencia masiva" or high risk. Art. 3 requires staff trained in DEA use during all opening hours and RCP training under **Ley 3665**. It was complemented by **Ley 5053/14** — [Defensoría del Pueblo CABA, Ley 4077](https://defen-dev.defensoria.org.ar/normativas-cdh/ley-4077-obligatoriedad-de-contar-con-desfibrilador-externo-automatico-en-lugares-de-concurrencia-masiva/); [NECOD on Ley 4077 / Ley 27.159](https://necod.com.ar/2018/11/01/acceso-publico-a-la-desfibrilacion-ley-de-la-ciudad-de-buenos-aires-no-4-077/)
- A 2014 CABA bill aimed to put DEAs in all sports centres. **It is unconfirmed whether it became law** — [Perfil, 02/08/2014](https://www.perfil.com/noticias/ciencia/Buscan-que-todos-los-gimnasios-tengan-desfibriladores-cardiacos-20140802-0036.phtml)

### Inferences
- For T&C: require each member to complete and sign the **CABA declaración de aptitud física (PAR-Q-based, minor/adult versions)** at sign-up. Keep requesting a **medical certificate** when the questionnaire flags a risk or for higher-risk programs, as contractual policy (and as good evidence for the duty of safety). Keeping the client's uploaded "apto físico" is lawful and prudent but should not be described as "required by Ley 139" without verifying the current text. Asking members to re-declare at least once a year and whenever their health changes is a reasonable policy.
- Compliance gap to flag: the VIP sessions must be **supervised by a profesor/a de Educación Física with a CABA-recognized title**, and that person must be registered as "responsable" in the Registro de Gimnasios. If "el Zar" or the assistants lack that title, this is a direct Ley 139 gap. Ley 139 is a CABA rule for physical premises; it does not govern online coaching for clients elsewhere.
- DEA: whether a small studio is legally bound is unclear (Ley 27.159 adhesion status; Ley 4077 "concurrencia masiva" threshold). Still, Decreto 402/2022 names gyms explicitly. Having a DEA, trained staff and an emergency medical coverage service ("área protegida") is the safest course and strongly supports the duty-of-safety defense (see §4).

### Gaps
- The full current consolidated text of Ley 139 (after Leyes 2575, 5397, 6316, 6616) and Decreto 11/2022 could not be read. The CABA Boletín Oficial is blocked in this session. The art. 3 vs art. 10 inconsistency, the retention period for declarations, and whether the gym must keep them are unverified.
- No verified source found on Ley 139 requirements for **first-aid kits, emergency medical service coverage, or mandatory signage** (a CABA first-aid regulations page appeared in results but was not read: [prevencionemede.com.ar](https://prevencionemede.com.ar/notas/normativa_primeros_auxilios_caba.html)).
- The threshold for "concurrencia masiva" under CABA Ley 4077 / Ley 5053 was not found.
- No guidance found from the Cámara Argentina de Gimnasios or similar industry bodies.

## 2. Who may prescribe diets, and how a coach may talk about nutrition and supplements

### Takeaway
In CABA, the profession of licenciado en nutrición is reserved to degree holders under **Ley 24.301**, whose scope is the former Capital Federal (now CABA); per HCDN the law was never regulated (sin reglamentación). Prescribing *treatments* for illnesses without a title can amount to ejercicio ilegal under Código Penal art. 208. Supplements must fit **CAA art. 1381**. Neither labels nor advertising may make therapeutic claims, and supplement advertising is controlled by **ANMAT (Disp. 4980/2005)**.

### Cited Findings
- Ley 24.301: "El ejercicio de la profesión de licenciado en nutrición en el ámbito de la Capital Federal queda sujeto a las disposiciones de la presente ley". The practice is authorized only to holders of the licenciado en nutrición title. Licensees may not delegate functions exclusive to the profession to unqualified staff, nor lend their signature or name to third parties — [Ley 24.301, texto original](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-24301-697/texto); [Derecho en Zapatillas, "Nutricionistas legales"](https://www.derechoenzapatillas.com/2015/nutricionistas-legales/)
- Ley 24.301 **lacks regulation** (sin reglamentación), and bills have proposed repealing or replacing it (e.g., expte. 1971-D-2017; a 2023 AADYND-backed bill, 3657-D-2023). Their outcome is unverified — [HCDN, expte. 1971-D-2017](https://www.hcdn.gob.ar/comisiones/permanentes/clgeneral/proyecto.html?exp=1971-D-2017); [AADYND bill PDF](https://www.aadynd.org.ar/descargas/archivos/nuevo-proyecto-setiembre-3657-d-2023.pdf)
- Código Penal art. 208 inc. 1 punishes anyone who, "sin título ni autorización para el ejercicio de un arte de curar … anunciare, prescribiere, administrare o aplicare habitualmente medicamentos … o cualquier medio destinado al tratamiento de las enfermedades de las personas, aun a título gratuito" — [Código Penal, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/15000-19999/16546/texact.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**
- CAA art. 1381 inc. 9: "En el rótulo, como en la publicidad de los suplementos dietarios no deberán figurar indicaciones terapéuticas atribuibles". Supplement advertising must fit the art. 1381 definition — [ANMAT, Disposición 4980/2005 (consolidated)](https://www.anmat.gob.ar/webanmat/Legislacion/NormasGenerales/Disposicion_ANMAT_4980-2005.pdf); [Legisalud, Disp. 4980/2005](https://e-legis-ar.msal.gov.ar/htdocs/legisalud/migration/html/1049.html)
- Under Disp. 4980/2005, supplement ads must not provoke fear or anxiety about health, induce indiscriminate use, present the product as a meal replacement, or include the phrase "venta libre" — [ANMAT Disp. 4980/2005](https://www.anmat.gob.ar/webanmat/Legislacion/NormasGenerales/Disposicion_ANMAT_4980-2005.pdf); [ANMAT, "¿Qué son los suplementos dietarios?"](https://www.anmat.gob.ar/Alimentos/suplementos_dietarios_prensa.pdf)
- ANMAT has banned weight-loss supplements sold online (Disp. 6545/2023, a matcha slimming supplement; Disp. 6506/2023, ten products promoted on online sales platforms) — [Página12](https://www.pagina12.com.ar/581059-la-anmat-prohibio-la-venta-de-un-suplemento-alimenticio-para/); [La Gaceta](https://www.lagaceta.com.ar/nota/1003699/sociedad/anmat-prohibio-venta-suplemento-para-bajar-peso.html)
- Courts have held that ANMAT has specific competence over advertising of these health products, not the Secretaría de Comercio — [Diario Judicial](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-74353-la-publicidad-enganosa-de-salud-no-es-tema-de-la-secretaria-de-comercio)
- A fine of $200,000 was confirmed against a supplement marketed as helping weight loss (it could not make that claim while registered as a supplement rather than a medicine). This was a pre-2019 precedent — [Derecho en Zapatillas, "Publicidad de remedios y suplementos dietarios"](https://www.derechoenzapatillas.com/2015/publicidad-de-remedios/)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: describe the service as "orientación y recomendaciones generales de hábitos alimentarios para acompañar el entrenamiento, con fines informativos y educativos". State that it is **not** a "plan alimentario, dieta, prescripción nutricional ni tratamiento"; that the coach is **not a licenciado en nutrición nor médico**; that it does not replace consultation with one; and that clients with any condition (diabetes, ED history, pregnancy, kidney disease, medication) must consult a médico or licenciado en nutrición before following it. Avoid the words "plan nutricional", "dieta personalizada" or "prescribir". If a nutritionist is added, they must design and sign the meal plans under their own matrícula and not lend their name.
- Supplements and the drinks bar (pre-workout, aminos, whey): only sell or recommend products registered with ANMAT/the food authority (RNPA/RNE number on label). Make **no therapeutic, fat-loss or "curative" claims**, and do not present them as meal replacements. Include warnings to consult a doctor (pregnancy, minors, hypertension, caffeine sensitivity for pre-workouts). Do not serve pre-workout or stimulant products to minors (policy recommendation, not a verified legal rule).
- The gym is a supplier in the chain (LDC art. 40) for drinks it sells. That is a further reason to keep supplier invoices and lot traceability (inference).

### Gaps
- The exact definition in Ley 24.301 of acts reserved to nutritionists (e.g., whether "plan de alimentación para personas sanas" is exclusive) was not verified. No case law or Colegio resolution was found on personal trainers giving meal plans.
- The specific Disp. 4980/2005 annex wording on weight-loss and performance claims for supplements was not read in full.
- Whether the current CAA art. 1381 contains specific rules for sports supplements or "suplementos para deportistas" (amino acids, pre-workout caffeine limits) was not verified.

## 3. Health records: the traumatólogo and Ley 26.529; separating clinical data from gym data

### Takeaway
When a licensed physician (traumatólogo) attends clients, that care is a health act. **Ley 26.529** (Derechos del Paciente, Historia Clínica y Consentimiento Informado) then applies: the patient owns the historia clínica, the doctor or health establishment is its custodian, records are confidential and kept for at least 10 years. The gym's body-composition data is not a historia clínica, but it is **health data, i.e., sensitive data under Ley 25.326**.

### Cited Findings
- Ley 26.529 art. 2 lists patient rights, including confidentiality (inc. d) and autonomy. Art. 12 defines the historia clínica as the obligatory, chronological, dated and complete document recording every act performed by health professionals. Art. 14 says the **patient is the owner (titular)** of the historia clínica. Art. 18 makes health establishments and professionals the **custodians (depositarios)**, who must keep it for a **minimum of 10 years** (prescripción liberatoria). Art. 19 lists who may request it. Informed consent is in arts. 5–7 (amended by Ley 26.742) — [Ley 26.529, texto actualizado, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/160000-164999/160432/texact.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**
- Ley 25.326 art. 2 defines "datos sensibles" as including "información referente a la salud". Art. 7: nobody may be compelled to provide sensitive data. Art. 8: health establishments and professionals may process patients' health data subject to professional secrecy. Art. 4 requires data to be adequate, relevant and not excessive for the purpose. Art. 5 requires free, express and informed consent. Art. 6 sets the information duties. Art. 9 requires security and confidentiality. Art. 10 sets a duty of confidentiality. Art. 21 requires databases to be registered — [Ley 25.326, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/60000-64999/64790/norma.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**
- The AAIP's binding "criterios orientadores" (Res. 4/2019) cite health information as the paradigm of data whose processing can be discriminatory (Criterio 4) — [AAIP Res. 4/2019, InfoLEG PDF](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/315000-319999/318874/res4AAIP.pdf)

### Inferences
- Structure the traumatólogo as an **independent health professional** (own matrícula, own historia clínica, own informed-consent forms, own malpractice insurance) who sees clients at the gym, not as a gym "service" whose notes sit in the gym's CRM. The historia clínica stays with the doctor. The gym receives only what the client authorizes (e.g., "apto / restricciones para entrenar") by **separate written consent**. The T&C should say the doctor is independent, that the gym is not responsible for medical acts, and that the doctor processes clinical data as an independent controller under Ley 26.529 and Ley 25.326 art. 8.
- Body-composition evaluations (weight, body fat %, visceral fat, BMR, "biological age"), progress photos, the uploaded apto físico and the PAR-Q declaration are **health data**. They need express written consent stating purpose, restricted staff access, security measures, a retention period, and registration of the database with the AAIP (Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos).
- "Biological age" and visceral-fat figures from a bioimpedance scale should be labeled as **estimates for training purposes, not medical diagnoses**. This limits misleading-claim and malpractice-type exposure (inference).

### Gaps
- No source found applying Ley 26.529 specifically to doctors practicing inside gyms, or on whether anthropometric evaluations by a non-health professional count as health acts.
- Interaction with Ley 27.706 (national digital clinical records program, 2023) was not researched.

## 4. Civil liability for injuries; which disclaimers are valid and which are void

### Takeaway
Argentine courts treat the gym–member relationship as a **consumer relationship**. They apply the **duty of safety** (LDC arts. 5–6 and 40, Constitución Nacional art. 42) as an objective obligation of result: the member proves the damage and the causal link, and the gym must prove an exonerating cause (victim's exclusive fault, third party, force majeure). Clauses that exclude or limit liability for personal injury are **void** under CCyC art. 1743 and LDC art. 37. Accepting "inherent risks" of exercise does not exempt the gym (CCyC art. 1719). Gyms win when they show the injury came from the client's own conduct or unforeseeable events and that facilities and supervision were adequate.

### Cited Findings
**Statutory framework**
- CCyC art. 1743: exemption or limitation clauses are invalid when they affect non-waivable rights, violate good faith, good morals or mandatory laws, or are abusive; they are also invalid if they release in advance, totally or partially, liability for damage caused by the debtor's dolo — [dateas, art. 1743 CCyC comentado](https://www.dateas.com/en-us/derecho/articulo_1743_del_Codigo_+Civil_+y_Comercial_+comentado); [CCyC, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/235000-239999/235975/norma.htm)
- LDC (Ley 24.240) art. 37: clauses that denature obligations or limit liability for damages are treated as not agreed ("se tendrán por no convenidas"), and interpretation must favor the consumer — [Proconsumer, art. 37](https://proconsumer.org.ar/art-37/); [Ley 24.240 texto actualizado, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/638/texact.htm)
- CCyC art. 1719 (asunción de riesgos): voluntary exposure to danger does not justify the harm or exempt from liability, unless it qualifies as the victim's own act that breaks the causal link. CCyC arts. 1757–1758: strict liability for risky things and activities, applicable to the owner, keeper and whoever performs or profits from the activity. Art. 1722: objective liability factor. Art. 1729: the victim's own act as defense. Arts. 988 and 1117–1122: abusive clauses in adhesion and consumer contracts — [CCyC, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/235000-239999/235975/norma.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**
- LDC art. 5 (services must not pose danger to consumers' health or physical integrity when used normally), art. 6 (risky services must follow safety rules and instructions), art. 40 (joint liability of the supply chain for damage from the risk or defect of the thing or service), art. 52 bis (punitive damages) — [Ley 24.240, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/638/texact.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**; the doctrinal basis of the gym duty of safety in LDC arts. 5, 6 and 40 is confirmed in [iProfesional, "cuándo un gimnasio debe hacerse cargo de los daños"](https://www.iprofesional.com/legales/301989-responsabilidad-ley-justicia-Fallo-cuando-duenos-de-gimnasio-son-responsables-por-danos-de-clientes)

**Case law (summaries)**
- *Cámara Civil, Neuquén:* the member–gym consumer relationship creates an accessory duty of safety with an objective liability factor. The gym owner bears an obligation of result, so the victim proves only the damage and the causal link, not fault — [Microjuris Al Día, 20/03/2017](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2017/03/20/relacion-de-consumo-entre-el-afiliado-que-se-lesiono-en-una-maquina-y-el-gimnasio/)
- *Cámara Nacional en lo Civil:* the supplier must prove an exonerating cause (break in the causal link). Liability is presumed in its capacity as gym owner; a judgment was confirmed for a member injured by a machine with a manufacturing defect — [Microjuris Al Día, 07/10/2019, "La belleza es dura"](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2019/10/07/la-belleza-es-dura-responsabilidad-del-titular-del-gimnasio-por-los-danos-que-sufrio-un-hombre-en-la-maquina-de-musculacion/)
- *Cámara Nacional en lo Civil (recent):* a gym chain was ordered to pay about $85M to a client who lost a finger, with the incapacity award raised to $55M. The court rejected punitive damages but held enforceable the clause limiting insurance coverage to $30M. It reasoned that the consumer duty of safety requires the supplier to guarantee users' indemnity, and that **accepting the risks inherent in physical activity does not exempt the gym when damage comes from factors outside the victim's conduct** — [iProfesional, "Fallo clave por accidente en un gimnasio"](https://www.iprofesional.com/legales/450857-fallo-clave-gimnasio-pagara-indemnizacion-millonaria-por-accidente-cliente)
- *CNCiv Sala F (July 2025):* reversed a judgment against two Buenos Aires gyms. LDC applied, but the injury was caused by the plaintiff tripping over another student, and **no facility failures or breach of the duty of safety were proven** — [Infobae, 16/07/2025](https://www.infobae.com/judiciales/2025/07/16/revocaron-una-condena-contra-un-gimnasio-por-la-caida-de-una-mujer-durante-una-clase-de-entrenamiento/)
- *Claim rejected:* a fall from a handrail was caused by the plaintiff's own conduct, not deficient structure or negligence — [Microjuris Al Día, 20/09/2021](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2021/09/20/fallos-relacion-de-consumo-corresponde-rechazar-la-demanda-de-danos-ya-que-la-caida-de-un-pasamanos-en-el-gimnasio-fue-producto-de-su-propio-accionar-y-no-por-una-estructura-deficiente-o-negligenc/)
- *Corrientes (May 2026):* a gym was held liable for a client's fracture during training. Liability stands unless a causa ajena or the victim's exclusive fault is shown — [El Litoral Corrientes, 08/05/2026](https://www.ellitoral.com.ar/sociedad/2026-5-8-16-48-0-condenan-a-un-gimnasio-de-corrientes-por-la-fractura-que-sufrio-una-clienta); [Tiempo de Seguros](https://tiempodeseguros.com/notas/corrientes-condenaron-un-gimnasio-por-la-fractura-que-sufrio-una-clienta-durante-un-entrenamiento)
- *Administrative:* CNACAF Sala IV confirmed an LDC fine against a gym for charging a fee higher than agreed, showing that gym contract clauses are policed — [Microjuris Al Día, 04/05/2016](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2016/05/04/se-multa-a-un-gimnasio-por-cobrar-una-cuota-superior-a-la-convenida-en-violacion-a-la-ley-de-defensa-del-consumidor/)
- A 2026 blog refers to an official list of 21 abusive clauses (**"Disposición 377/2026"**). It was **not verified** in official sources — [mariovadillo.com.ar](https://mariovadillo.com.ar/21-clausulas-abusivas-que-tu-contrato-no-puede-tener-aunque-las-hayas-firmado/)

### Inferences
- **Void or high-risk clauses:** "El gimnasio no se responsabiliza por lesiones / accidentes / daños a la salud", "el socio renuncia a reclamar", "el cliente entrena bajo su exclusivo riesgo", caps on personal-injury damages, and waivers of the consumer forum. Under LDC art. 37 these are treated as not written. In administrative proceedings they can also draw fines as abusive clauses.
- **Meaningful clauses (they allocate duties and build evidence rather than waive liability):**
  - the client's duty to give truthful health information (PAR-Q, medications, injuries) and update it;
  - a duty to follow staff instructions, to warm up, to report pain or symptoms immediately and stop;
  - no use of equipment without instruction;
  - informed acknowledgment of the inherent risks of exercise, framed as information, not waiver;
  - the gym's own safety undertakings: supervision by a qualified profesor, equipment maintenance, emergency protocol, DEA, emergency medical coverage;
  - a recommendation to take personal accident insurance;
  - no liability for lost personal belongings that are not deposited, with care (it is still subject to LDC scrutiny);
  - online coaching: the client trains without in-person supervision, must use safe equipment and space, and must stop on symptoms.

  These help prove the victim's own fault (CCyC art. 1729) or a broken causal link, which is how gyms won in the CNCiv Sala F and 2021 cases.
- Civil liability insurance is critical. The CNCiv case upheld an insurance coverage cap as enforceable against the victim, but the gym itself remains liable above it.
- Keep evidence: signed PAR-Q, attendance logs, training plans, equipment maintenance logs, staff titles, DEA and first-aid training certificates.

### Gaps
- Full judgment citations (carátula, expte., date) for the CNCiv $85M case and the Sala F 2025 case were not obtained.
- No Argentine case found specifically on online or remote coaching injuries, or on liability for injuries linked to supplement or nutrition advice from a coach.

## 5. Image rights: client before/after photos, testimonials, social media, revocation, minors

### Takeaway
Using a client's image needs **consent** (CCyC art. 53). That consent is **not presumed, is interpreted restrictively (limited to the stated purpose), and is freely revocable** (CCyC art. 55). For commercial use of a portrait, Ley 11.723 art. 31 requires **express** consent and allows revocation with compensation for damages. Body-transformation photos linked to weight or body-fat results are also likely health data under Ley 25.326. Images of minors carry extra protection.

### Cited Findings
- CCyC art. 53: capturing or reproducing a person's image or voice requires consent, except (a) participation in public acts, (b) prevailing scientific, cultural or educational interest with precautions to avoid unnecessary harm, (c) regular exercise of the right to report on matters of general interest — [leyfacil, art. 53 CCyC](https://leyfacil.com.ar/codigo-civil-y-comercial/articulo-53/); [Infojus CCyC comentado, art. 53](https://universojus.com/ccc-comentado-infojus/introduccion-art-53/)
- CCyC art. 55: "El consentimiento para la disposición de los derechos personalísimos es admitido si no es contrario a la ley, la moral o las buenas costumbres. Este consentimiento no se presume, es de interpretación restrictiva, y libremente revocable." Doctrine says art. 55 applies to the image right even though art. 53 says nothing about revocation — [leyfacil, art. 55](https://leyfacil.com.ar/?p=210); [Microjuris, "La protección de la imagen en el ámbito virtual"](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2022/06/22/doctrina-la-proteccion-de-la-imagen-en-el-ambito-virtual/)
- Consent is "estrictamente limitado a la finalidad o circunstancias en que se presta". Silence is not consent — [Microjuris doctrine](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2022/06/22/doctrina-la-proteccion-de-la-imagen-en-el-ambito-virtual/)
- Ley 11.723 art. 31: a person's photographic portrait cannot be put on the market without that person's express consent. Whoever gave consent may revoke it, compensating damages — [Ley 11.723, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**; the revocation-with-damages rule is also summarized in [Escuela de la Magistratura, CCyC – Privacidad en la Web (PDF)](https://www.escuelamagistratura.gov.ar/JyE/leyes/Codigo%20Civil%20y%20Comercial%20de%20la%20Nacion%20-%20Privacidad%20en%20la%20Web.pdf)
- CNCiv Sala B held a news portal liable for publishing photos taken from social networks without consent. An image being accessible on social media does not authorize third-party publication — [Diario Judicial, "Derecho a la imagen en redes sociales de empresas"](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-86393-derecho-a-la-imagen-en-redes-sociales-de-empresas)
- When a company reuses a person's photo or video for promotion, consent must be obtained — [Diario Judicial](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-86393-derecho-a-la-imagen-en-redes-sociales-de-empresas)
- CSJN case-law digest on the image right (Aug 2025) — [CSJN Secretaría de Jurisprudencia, "Derecho a la imagen", agosto 2025](https://sj.csjn.gov.ar/homeSJ/notas/nota/224/documento) (not read in detail)
- Ley 26.061 art. 22: children and adolescents have the right to respect for their dignity, reputation and image. Exposing or disseminating images that identify them against their or their parents' will is prohibited when it harms their dignity or reputation — [Ley 26.061, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/110000-114999/110778/norma.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**

### Inferences
- **Before/after and testimonial consent should be:**
  - a **separate, specific, written (or click-wrap) opt-in**, never bundled into the general T&C and never a condition of membership;
  - specific about which images or videos, which channels (web, Instagram, TikTok, paid ads), whether the face is shown, whether name, age or results figures (kg lost, % fat) appear, the territory, and the duration;
  - explicit that consent is **revocable at any time** (CCyC art. 55). On revocation the gym removes the content from its own website and accounts and stops new uses within a set short period. The T&C cannot make revocation conditional on a penalty, although Ley 11.723 art. 31 lets the gym claim actual damages for commercial reliance (e.g., printed materials). It is safer to waive that or limit it to proven out-of-pocket costs.
- Progress photos taken for internal tracking are a different purpose from marketing. Using them in ads without a specific consent breaches the restrictive-interpretation rule.
- Because transformation photos combined with body metrics reveal health information, treat them as **sensitive data**: express consent under Ley 25.326 arts. 5 and 7, secure storage, restricted access, and deletion when no longer needed.
- Minors: no public before/after of minors is recommended. If unavoidable, get the written consent of both parents or legal representatives **plus the adolescent's own agreement**, and avoid any body-shaming or weight-focused framing (Ley 26.061 art. 22).
- Online clients abroad: consent forms should also cover cross-border publication (other researchers cover international data transfer).

### Gaps
- No Argentine case law found specifically on gyms' or coaches' use of client before/after photos, or on damages after revocation of advertising-image consent.

## 6. Biometric access control (fingerprint reader)

### Takeaway
No AAIP decision, guidance or Argentine consumer case specific to gym fingerprint readers was found. Under **AAIP Res. 4/2019 (Criterio 4)**, biometric data are personal data but count as **sensitive only when they can reveal additional information with discriminatory potential** (e.g., health, ethnicity). A fingerprint template used only for access is therefore normally ordinary personal data, still subject to consent, information, proportionality and security duties. Spanish AEPD fines against gyms are useful comparators that favor offering a non-biometric alternative.

### Cited Findings
- AAIP Res. 4/2019, Criterio 4, defines biometric data as personal data obtained by specific technical processing of physical, physiological or behavioral traits that allow unique identification. They are sensitive **only** if they can reveal additional data whose use may be discriminatory (e.g., ethnic origin, health). The "criterios orientadores" are **mandatory** for those subject to Ley 25.326 — [AAIP Res. 4/2019, InfoLEG PDF](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/315000-319999/318874/res4AAIP.pdf); [abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/mejores-practicas-en-la-aplicacion-de-la-ley-de-proteccion-de-datos-personales-resolucion-42019-de-la-agencia-de-acceso-a-la-informacion-publica/23000); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/argentina/privacy/nuevos-criterios-orientadores-e-indicadores-de-mejores-pr%C3%A1cticas-en-la-aplicaci%C3%B3n-de-la-ley-n%C2%B0-25326)
- Academic critique: Res. 4/2019 defines biometric data but sets no special regime for them — [UBA thesis, "Protección de datos biométricos en Argentina"](http://bibliotecadigital.econ.uba.ar/download/tpos/1502-1593_MayerVI.pdf)
- A later InfoLEG norm cites Res. 4/2019 and appears to broaden "sensitive data" to include genetic and biometric data. Its identity, scope and status were **not verified** — [InfoLEG 376740](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/375000-379999/376740/norma.htm)
- Ley 25.326 art. 4 (data adequate, relevant and not excessive), art. 5 (consent), art. 6 (information), art. 9 (security), art. 21 (database registration) — [Ley 25.326, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/60000-64999/64790/norma.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**
- *Comparative (Spain, AEPD):* a gym was fined €50,000 (reduced to €27,000 with early payment) for using fingerprints for access. Grounds: less intrusive viable alternatives existed (e.g., a personal card), fingerprints of former members had not been deleted, and members were not clearly informed — [Gym Factory, 14/02/2024](https://gymfactory.net/2024/02/14/la-aepd-multa-a-un-gimnasio-con-27-000e-por-exigir-datos-biometricos-para-acceder-al-centro/); [Genbeta](https://www.genbeta.com/actualidad/que-tu-gimnasio-te-pida-tu-huella-dactilar-excesivo-multa-aepd-no-justificar-bien-su-uso-existiendo-alternativas); [Idealex](https://idealex.press/datos-biometricos-gimnasio-huella-dactilar-aepd/)

### Inferences
- Practices that comply with Argentine law and match the comparative standard:
  - **offer a non-biometric alternative** (card, QR/app code, PIN or reception check-in) so consent is genuinely free;
  - get express, informed consent at enrolment, with a privacy notice saying the reader stores a template, not an image, if that is true;
  - store templates locally and encrypted, never combined with health data;
  - **delete templates when the membership ends** (and on request);
  - include the access database in the AAIP registration;
  - require the vendor to sign a data-processing agreement (Ley 25.326 art. 25).
- If Argentina's pending data-protection reform, or Convention 108+ ratified by Ley 27.699, ends up treating biometric data as a special category, the alternative-plus-consent approach already complies (inference; status not verified).

### Gaps
- No AAIP sanction or opinion on biometric access in gyms or private premises was found, and no Argentine consumer case either.
- The status of any Argentine data-protection bill as of Nov 2026 and of Convention 108+ entry into force was not verified here; other researchers may cover it.

## 7. Minors as gym members

### Takeaway
No CABA statutory minimum age for gyms was found. Ley 139 (as amended by Ley 6316) contemplates **separate declaración de aptitud física forms for minors**. Contractual capacity and consent follow the CCyC: under-18s are minors, and parents or legal representatives must contract and consent. Adolescents 13–16 can consent to non-invasive health-related acts, and 16+ are treated as adults for decisions about care of their own body. Age limits used in the market are private policies (e.g., 14+, or 12–16 with authorization).

### Cited Findings
- Ley 6316 amended Ley 139 art. 3 to provide differentiated declaration forms for minors and adults — [RTN](https://rtn.com.ar/eliminan-la-obligacion-de-presentar-un-certificado-medico-para-ingresar-a-los-gimnasios-en-caba/); [Infobae 2020](https://www.infobae.com/sociedad/2020/07/13/analizaran-eliminar-el-requisito-de-tener-un-permiso-medico-para-ir-al-gimnasio-en-caba/)
- Minors must attend the medical fitness exam with a responsible adult, and at least one parent should sign alongside the doctor for minors' certifications (practice guidance) — [docmed.ar](https://docmed.ar/medicina-del-deporte-apto-fisico-marco-legal/)
- Market practice: one club sets 14 as the minimum age for its gym areas; SportClub's CABA program admits 12–16-year-olds with the main member's authorization — [Campestre gym rules PDF](https://campestreags.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/reglamento-gimnasio.pdf); [Sindicato del Seguro / SportClub](https://www.sindicatodelseguro.com.ar/sportclub/); [saludymovimiento.com.ar](https://saludymovimiento.com.ar/gimnasio-menores/)
- CCyC art. 25 (minor: under 18; adolescent: 13–18). Art. 26: minors act through representatives. Adolescents 13–16 are presumed able to decide on non-invasive treatments that do not seriously compromise health. From 16 the adolescent is considered an adult for decisions on care of their own body. Art. 101 (representation by parents). Art. 684 (low-value everyday contracts by a minor are presumed made with parents' consent) — [CCyC, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/235000-239999/235975/norma.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**; see also [Garrahan compilation, CCyC menores](https://garrahan.gov.ar/images/docencia/investigacion/marcos_regulatorios/normativa_vigente_republica_argentina/codigo-civil_menores.docx)

### Inferences
- T&C clause:
  - members under 18 are admitted only with a parent or legal representative signing the enrolment, the minor's PAR-Q declaration, and the health-data consent (an "autorización y declaración del responsable");
  - recommend a pediatric or sports-medicine certificate for minors;
  - set a minimum age (e.g., 14), with 14–17-year-olds only under direct profesor supervision and age-appropriate loads;
  - no stimulant pre-workouts or supplement "suggestions" to minors;
  - no publication of minors' images, or only with dual parental consent plus the minor's assent.
- Online coaching for minors: same parental-consent gate, plus verification of the parent's identity (inference).

### Gaps
- No CABA rule found setting a minimum gym age or specific supervision ratios for minors; Ley 139 full text not read.

## 8. Advertising: health, weight-loss and "transformation" claims

### Takeaway
General advertising is governed by **DNU 274/2019 (Lealtad Comercial)**, which replaced Ley 22.802. Its art. 11 bans advertising that through inaccuracies or omissions may mislead about characteristics, quality, results or other aspects of services, with fines up to 10,000,000 Unidades Móviles and corrective advertising. Health-product (supplement) claims fall under ANMAT, and statements in ads bind the supplier (LDC art. 8). No Argentine sanction specific to gym before/after ads was found.

### Cited Findings
- DNU 274/2019 art. 11 prohibits "cualquier clase de presentación, de publicidad o propaganda que mediante inexactitudes u ocultamientos pueda inducir a error, engaño o confusión" about the characteristics, quality, price or other aspects of goods or services. Art. 57: sanctions from warning up to fines of 1 to 10,000,000 Unidades Móviles. The authority can also order rectification of the advertising. The Secretaría de Comercio Interior enforces it ex officio or on complaint — [DNU 274/2019, texto original](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/decreto-274-2019-322236/texto); [Boletín Oficial 22/04/2019](https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/205888/20190422); [Abeledo Gottheil, DNU 274/2019 vs Ley 22.802](https://abeledogottheil.com.ar/nuevo-regimen-de-leatad-comercial-similitudes-y-diferencias-entre-las-disposiciones-del-dnu-274-2019-y-la-derogada-ley-22-802/); [Mercojuris](https://mercojuris.com/lealtad-comercial-dnu-2742019-por-dra-maria-paula-guinazu/)
- DNU art. 11 reproduces former Ley 22.802 art. 9, so pre-2019 case law stays relevant. **Ley 22.802 is repealed** — [Abeledo Gottheil](https://abeledogottheil.com.ar/nuevo-regimen-de-leatad-comercial-similitudes-y-diferencias-entre-las-disposiciones-del-dnu-274-2019-y-la-derogada-ley-22-802/)
- Procedure: Res. 248/2019 allows proceedings to start from an inspection record (acta de constatación) — [Res. 248/2019](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/resoluci%C3%B3n-248-2019-323502/texto)
- Supplements: no therapeutic claims in labels or advertising (CAA art. 1381 inc. 9; ANMAT Disp. 4980/2005); ANMAT, not Comercio, controls this advertising — [ANMAT Disp. 4980/2005](https://www.anmat.gob.ar/webanmat/Legislacion/NormasGenerales/Disposicion_ANMAT_4980-2005.pdf); [Diario Judicial](https://www.diariojudicial.com/news-74353-la-publicidad-enganosa-de-salud-no-es-tema-de-la-secretaria-de-comercio)
- LDC art. 8: specifics stated in advertising bind the offeror and form part of the contract — [Ley 24.240, InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/638/texact.htm) **[statute, not re-fetched]**

### Inferences
- Transformation marketing should:
  - not promise or guarantee results ("baja 10 kg en 30 días", "garantizado"), because LDC art. 8 turns such promises into contractual obligations and DNU art. 11 exposes the gym to fines;
  - show only **real, unretouched** client photos with consent;
  - disclose that results vary and depend on adherence, nutrition, genetics and medical condition;
  - disclose the time frame, and whether other interventions (nutritionist, medical treatment, supplements) were involved;
  - avoid medical claims ("cura", "trata", "reduce el riesgo de diabetes") and avoid presenting "biological age" as a medical diagnosis;
  - keep supplement mentions free of fat-loss or therapeutic claims;
  - not target minors with weight-loss messaging.
- Website T&C should state that testimonials are individual experiences and not typical or guaranteed outcomes. That disclosure does not cure a misleading main claim (inference based on DNU art. 11 covering "ocultamientos").

### Gaps
- No Argentine sanction found specific to gym or coach before/after advertising or influencer testimonials. Whether Ley 26.396 (eating disorders) imposes mandatory legends on weight-loss diet or treatment ads was not verified. Any 2024–2026 changes to DNU 274/2019 (e.g., deregulation decrees) were not verified.
